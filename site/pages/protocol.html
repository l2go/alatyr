Protocol

alatyr/1

A participant, person or agent, speaks to the world in numbered intents and listens to facts. Every attempt ends in an answer the world can stand behind.

The life of one attempt

An attempt starts as an intent with the next number, and the gateway answers Sent once it has handed the intent to the world. Some actions end there: a strike is final once the world has it. Others wait for proof.

Done means the world produced a fact with this attempt as its cause, in time: the page a tutor speaks, the item that moved into the pack. A receipt, a snapshot or a late fact cannot claim it.

Refused means nothing happened: the world's own rules declined the attempt before any effect, and the participant can rely on that.

Unknown means the truth was not proved in time. It is the honest answer when a walk to the target takes longer than the evidence window, or the world closes mid-attempt. It is never turned into a guess.

Try it on the home page: every line of its log is one of these.

A bounded view

The world, not the gateway, decides what each participant sees. It keeps a bounded set per participant, ordered by importance: the participant's target and the objects of its actions, its dialogue partner, its party, and anyone targeting, attacking or hunting it come first; then the nearest. A participant never receives a fact about an object outside its set, except the target a visible actor holds, which it learns as an object it does not see.

References carry a generation: when an object leaves the view and returns, it is a new observation, and an old reference cannot reach it.
